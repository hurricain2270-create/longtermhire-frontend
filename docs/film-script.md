# The film, second draft

Aimed at the owner or engineer who decides which hire company to use. He has
hired plant before and does not need convincing to hire — he needs a reason to
move. So the argument is not capital, it is what happens after he signs.

Roughly two minutes. He can turn it off.

---

## 1 — Cold open

*Black. A phone ringing out.*

**You need a machine on Monday.**

*Beat.*

**So you ring around.**

---

## 2 — The way it works now

*Simple animation. A phone. Then the same phone again. Then again.*

**Someone will get back to you.**

**Might be an hour. Might be Thursday.**

*The phone appears a second time.*

**Then you ring again to check the rate.**

*A third time.*

**Then again to see if it is still available.**

*The phones stack up.*

**Every answer you want is in somebody else's head, and you have to ask for it.**

## 3 — The turn

*The dark clears. The portal appears — clean, dark, the fleet laid out.*

**Or you log in.**

*Pause here. Let the screen do the work.*

---

## 4 — What he can actually do

*Each line lands on the thing it describes, on screen.*

**Every machine we have. Photos, specs, hours, what it does.**

**What it costs, this month and every month after.**

**Move the slider. Twelve months instead of three, and watch the rate fall.**

**Generate your own quote. On our letterhead, with your figures, at eleven at
night if that is when you are working.**

*Small, underneath:*

**No phone call. No waiting on Wednesday.**

---

## 5 — The rate

*The schedule, month by month, the figure stepping down.*

**Every other supplier's rate goes one way.**

**Ours falls one per cent every month you keep the machine, and holds there.**

**You can see the whole term before you sign anything. Check the arithmetic.**

---

## 6 — When something goes wrong

*The fault flow, quickly. Photo from the seat. A card on a subbie's phone.*

**Your supervisor reports it from the machine. A photo, thirty seconds.**

**We have a fitter on the way before anyone has picked up a phone.**

**And you can watch it happen from your desk.**

*Small:*

**A tyre reported at twenty past eleven at night was sorted by midnight.**

---

## 7 — The claim

*Plain. No animation. Let it sit.*

**We have not seen another hire company in Australia doing this.**

**Not a website. Your own portal, your own rates, your own machines, open
whenever you want it.**

---

## 8 — The close

*The login screen.*

**Everything else stays the same. Same machines, same trucks, same blokes.**

**You just stop waiting for someone to ring you back.**

*Beat.*

**Have a look around.**

---

## Notes

- The phone call in section 2 is the whole argument. If it does not sting, he
  has a better supplier than we think and the rest will not land either.
- Section 7 is the only place we claim anything. Everything before it is
  demonstrable on screen while he watches.
- No mention of capital, depreciation or the cost of ownership. He settled that
  question before he ever spoke to us.
- The fault section is short on purpose. It matters to him because it is what
  his supervisor deals with, not because he will ever use it.
